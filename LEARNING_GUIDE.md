# 한글길 Learning Guide

**A complete guide to learning Korean with Hangeul-gil**

---

## 🎯 Your Success Path

This guide shows you exactly how to use Hangeul-gil to reach **conversation-level Korean in 11 weeks**.

### Timeline
- **Weeks 1 (Days 1–7):** Phase 0 — Master Hangul (Korean alphabet)
- **Weeks 2–4:** Phase 1 — Survival Korean (greetings, introductions, basic requests)
- **Weeks 5–10:** Phase 2 — Building sentences (grammar, particles, conjugation)
- **Weeks 11+:** Phase 3 — Real conversations (scenarios, dialogues)

## 📖 How to Use This App

### 1. **Start with Hangul (Phase 0)**

#### Step 1: Learn the Interactive Hangul Chart
- Go to **🎨 Learn Hangul**
- Tap each letter to:
  - Hear the correct pronunciation
  - Understand how the shape relates to mouth position
  - Read the pro tips

**Recommended pace:** 1–2 hours per day × 7 days

#### Step 2: Build Syllables
- Go to **🔤 Build Syllables**
- Combine consonants + vowels to see real syllables form
- Tap the buttons to hear each combination
- Notice how every syllable is a square block

**Why this matters:** Building syllables teaches you how Hangul actually works, not just memorizing shapes.

#### Step 3: Reading Drills
- Come back to **Learn Hangul**
- Practice reading 10–20 syllables per day
- Start with romanization hints (removes as you get faster)
- Goal: Read 30 random syllables correctly by end of week

---

### 2. **Learn Your First Words (Phase 1)**

#### Survival Phrases
After mastering Hangul, you'll learn these in order:
1. **Greetings** — 안녕하세요 (annyeong haseyo — hello)
2. **"I am" — 저는 학생이에요 (I am a student)**
3. **Simple possession — 시간 있어요 (I have time)**
4. **Numbers — 오천원 (5,000 won)**
5. **Polite requests — 물 주세요 (Water, please)**

#### The Explain → Do → Review Loop
Each lesson follows this pattern:

```
📖 EXPLANATION
   ↓
   "Here's why Korean uses particles..."
   ↓
💡 EXAMPLES with audio
   ↓
   Listen to 3–5 real examples
   ↓
✍️ EXERCISES with real recall
   ↓
   Type the Korean, listen to yourself
   ↓
🔄 REVIEW
   ↓
   System schedules this for spaced repetition
```

**Recommended pace:** 1 new lesson per day × 15–20 minutes

---

### 3. **Review with Spaced Repetition**

#### What is Spaced Repetition?
After you learn something, you're about to forget it at predictable times:
- **1 day** after learning
- **3 days** after
- **1 week** after
- **2 weeks** after

Reviewing at exactly these times locks knowledge in long-term memory.

#### Your Daily Review Queue
- Go to **🔄 Review** each day
- You'll see cards like:
  - Korean → English (너는 뭐예요? = What are you?)
  - English → Korean (I am happy. = ? )
  - Audio → Korean (hear sound, type in Hangul)

#### How to Grade Yourself
After answering, choose one:
- **Again** (❌) — Too hard, don't know
- **Hard** (😕) — Remembered, but slowly
- **Good** (✓) — Remembered correctly
- **Easy** (🎯) — Knew instantly

The system adjusts when to show you each card based on your feedback.

## 📚 The Curriculum

### Phase 0: Hangul (Days 1–7)
Master the Korean alphabet in one week.

| Unit | What You Learn |
|------|-----------------|
| 0.1 | Syllable structure (how blocks work) |
| 0.2 | Basic vowels: ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅣ (4 vowels) |
| 0.3 | First consonants: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ (4 consonants) |
| 0.4 | Reading first syllables: 가 나 다 라 (4 syllables) |
| 0.5 | Y-vowels: ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ (add "y" sound) |
| 0.6 | Aspirated consonants: ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ (with air) |
| 0.7 | Tense consonants: ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ (tight, no air) |
| 0.8 | Compound vowels: ㅐ ㅔ ㅒ ㅖ (combinations) |
| 0.9 | Batchim (final consonants): 닭 (chicken) |
| 0.10 | Real words: 한국 (Korea), 서울 (Seoul), 커피 (coffee) |
| 0.11 | Typing Korean: 두벌식 (2-set keyboard) |

**Exit Check:** Read 30 random Hangul words aloud + type 10 dictated words correctly.

---

### Phase 1: Survival Korean (Weeks 2–4)

Learn the phrases you'll actually use traveling.

| Lesson | What You Learn | Example |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| 1.1 | Greetings + thanks | 안녕하세요. 감사합니다. |
| 1.2 | "I am X" | 저는 학생이에요. (I am a student.) |
| 1.3 | Topic marker (은/는) | 저는 미국 사람이에요. (As for me, I'm American.) |
| 1.4 | This/that/that over there | 이거 뭐예요? (What is this?) |
| 1.5 | Have/don't have | 시간 있어요? (Do you have time?) |
| 1.6 | Sino-Korean numbers | 오천원이에요. (It's 5,000 won.) |
| 1.7 | Native Korean numbers | 커피 두 잔 주세요. (Two coffees, please.) |
| 1.8 | Polite requests | 물 주세요. (Water, please.) |

**By the end:** You can introduce yourself, ask for things, and understand basic responses.

---

### Phase 2: Building Sentences (Weeks 5–10)

Learn the grammar foundations for real conversation.

| Lesson | What You Learn | Example |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| 2.1 | Subject marker (이/가) | 날씨가 좋아요. (The weather is nice.) |
| 2.2 | Object marker (을/를) | 밥을 먹어요. (I eat rice.) |
| 2.3 | Present polite (-아요/-어요) | 가요, 먹어요, 공부해요 |
| 2.4 | Negation (안) | 안 먹어요. (I don't eat it.) |
| 2.5 | Location (에 vs 에서) | 학교에 가요. (Go to school.) vs 카페에서 공부해요. (Study at café.) |
| 2.6 | Past tense (-았어요/-었어요) | 어제 영화를 봤어요. (I watched a movie yesterday.) |
| 2.7 | Want/would like | 한국에 가고 싶어요. (I want to go to Korea.) |
| 2.8 | Future/plans | 내일 친구를 만날 거예요. (I'm going to meet my friend tomorrow.) |
| 2.9 | Linking words (and, but) | 밥을 먹고 커피를 마셨어요. (I ate and drank coffee.) |
| 2.10 | Can/can't | 한국어를 읽을 수 있어요. (I can read Korean.) |

**By the end:** You can form basic sentences, use past/future tense, and explain what you can/can't do.

---

### Phase 3: Real Conversations (Weeks 11+)

Put it all together in real-world scenarios.

| Scenario | What You Practice |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Meeting someone** | Introduce yourself, ask where they're from, what they do |
| **At a café** | Order drinks, ask about menu items, make small talk |
| **Shopping** | Ask prices, ask sizes, negotiate (politely) |
| **Taking the subway** | Ask for directions, buy a ticket, ask what stop is next |
| **Making plans** | Suggest activities, ask when they're free, confirm time/place |
| **Describing your weekend** | Talk about what you did, where you went, who you saw |

**Each scenario includes:**
1. **Dialogue** — Read & listen to a realistic conversation
2. **Breakdown** — Grammar and vocabulary explained
3. **Rebuild exercise** — You reconstruct the dialogue from English prompts
4. **Free practice** — Constrained to grammar you've learned

## 🎯 Study Tips for Success

### 1. **Daily Habit (20–30 min)**
- **Best time:** Morning or early evening
- **Setup:** Quiet place, phone on silent
- **Frequency:** Every day beats sporadic cramming

### 2. **Review is Key**
- Do your daily review queue **first** before new lessons
- Reviews should take 10–15 min per day
- This locks knowledge in long-term memory

### 3. **Speak Out Loud**
- Always read exercises aloud
- Listen to the audio afterward
- Compare your pronunciation to native speakers

### 4. **Don't Skip Explanations**
- Read the "why" behind each grammar point
- Understanding > memorizing
- This prevents you from getting stuck later

### 5. **Keep a Notebook**
- Write down words/phrases that feel tricky
- Handwriting activates different memory pathways
- Refer back when you get stuck

## 💡 Common Questions

### Q: What if I miss a day?
**A:** No problem! The app doesn't penalize you. Just pick it up the next day. Missing one day doesn't reset anything.

### Q: Can I learn faster by doing multiple lessons per day?
**A:** Not really. Daily review is more important than lesson speed. Quality > speed. Master Phase 0 before moving to Phase 1.

### Q: How much audio will there be?
**A:** Every word and sentence has audio. You'll hear native Korean pronunciation from day one, trained native speakers.

### Q: Will the app work offline?
**A:** Yes! After your first load, everything works offline. Perfect for planes, subways, commutes.

### Q: Is there an app for my phone?
**A:** Yes! After the web version launches, we'll make it installable as a progressive web app (PWA). Works like a native app.

### Q: How much does this cost?
**A:** Forever free. Open source. No ads, no accounts, no premium tier.
